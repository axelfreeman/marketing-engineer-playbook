# 03 — Scope and pricing

Publish both before the call. Ambiguity costs more than a lost lead: it buys a negotiation, and the negotiation
is usually about whether the work was ever specified.

## What good published scope looks like

- **In scope, as a list of artifacts**, not activities: "one offer page live on your domain, schema markup, one
  tracked event, a written kill rule for the first test."
- **Out of scope, explicitly**: ad spend management, content volume promises, hiring, and anything needing a
  login you have not handed over.
- **What the client owns at the end**: every artifact, inside their accounts, documented. No migration project
  at the end, because nothing was ever hosted on the vendor's side.
- **How the engagement ends**: notice period and what happens to the work. Fourteen days' notice and nothing
  breaks is the honest test of whether you were building an asset or renting attention.

## Pricing shape

Fixed packages beat hours: hours reward slowness and hide whether anything shipped. Three shapes cover most of
it — a one-off sprint for a single channel, a monthly block for build-measure-kill loops, and a full build for
the whole system handed over running.
